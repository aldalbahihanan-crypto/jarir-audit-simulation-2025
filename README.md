# Jarir Audit Simulation 2025

An educational external-audit simulation for **Jarir Marketing Company (Jarir Bookstore)**.

> **Important disclaimer:** This is an educational simulation, not an actual audit of Jarir Marketing Company. Public financial information is used only as financial context. All transaction-level revenue records, inventory items, identifiers, counts, and supporting evidence are fictional and illustrative.

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates the main stages of an external audit engagement:

- Audit planning
- Understanding the entity and its business environment
- Risk assessment
- Materiality calculation
- Revenue testing
- Inventory testing
- Audit findings documentation
- Executive audit summary
- Professional audit working-paper organization

The project was prepared as an accounting and external-audit portfolio project.

## Project Objectives

The objectives of this simulation are to demonstrate the ability to:

- Understand the audit process from planning through reporting.
- Identify and assess risks of material misstatement.
- Calculate overall materiality, performance materiality, and the clearly trivial threshold.
- Design audit procedures for selected financial statement accounts.
- Perform illustrative substantive testing.
- Document hypothetical exceptions and evidence limitations.
- Compare illustrative differences with materiality thresholds.
- Prepare professional audit documentation and recommendations.
- Use linked Excel formulas instead of unnecessary hardcoded calculations.

## Risk Assessment

The simulation identifies the following potential risks:

### Revenue Risks

- Revenue may be recorded in the wrong accounting period.
- Recorded revenue may not agree to supporting documentation.
- Revenue transactions may have incomplete fulfillment evidence.
- Sales returns, discounts, or other adjustments may be recorded incorrectly.

### Inventory Risks

- Recorded quantities may differ from physical quantities.
- Inventory values may be calculated incorrectly.
- Inventory may include slow-moving or damaged items.
- Inventory may be misstated because of incorrect cut-off around year-end.
- Inventory provisions may not reflect the lower of cost and net realizable value.

These are planning risk hypotheses and are not confirmed findings about Jarir.

## Materiality Methodology

Profit before zakat and income tax was selected as the benchmark because Jarir is a profitable listed company and earnings are relevant to financial statement users.

The following percentages are educational planning assumptions:

- Overall materiality percentage: 5.0%
- Performance materiality percentage: 75.0% of overall materiality
- Clearly trivial percentage: 5.0% of overall materiality

### Calculations

```text
Overall materiality
= Profit before zakat and income tax × 5.0%
Performance materiality
= Overall materiality × 75.0%
= SAR 53,867.85 × 75.0%
= SAR 40,400.89 thousand
Clearly trivial threshold
= Overall materiality × 5.0%
= SAR 53,867.85 × 5.0%
= SAR 2,693.39 thousand
These percentages are illustrative assumptions and are not universal audit requirements.

Overall Simulation Conclusion
The documented simulation identifies illustrative revenue and inventory exceptions requiring follow-up.
However, the project does not support an audit opinion on Jarir's actual financial statements because:
Transaction-level records were not available.
Supporting documents were fictional.
No access to management was available.
No actual inventory count was attended.
No actual audit engagement was performed.
The sample cannot be extrapolated to the complete population.
The appropriate conclusion is therefore limited to the educational scenarios documented in the workbook.
Recommendations for a Real Audit Engagement
In a real audit engagement, the following additional procedures would be appropriate:
Obtain actual revenue listings and test transactions before and after 31 December.
Agree revenue transactions to invoices, fulfillment evidence, and system records.
Investigate all amount differences and determine whether they are timing, pricing, return, or data-entry issues.
Attend or observe inventory counts at selected stores and warehouses.
Compare count sheets with inventory records.
Review inventory aging and slow-moving inventory reports.
Evaluate damaged inventory and net realizable value evidence.
Test purchases and dispatches around the reporting date.
Aggregate identified differences and compare them with materiality thresholds.
Obtain management explanations and document final conclusions.
